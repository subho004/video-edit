# Cinematic sequences and story structures (footage-agnostic)

Use when building the shot list: pick ONE story structure (A), assemble shots with the sequence blocks (B), then set rhythm,
sound and text (C). Works on any footage because it only reorders and times what exists.

**Evidence level.** Compiled from web research (blogs, tutorials, vendor guides, film-theory summaries). Structures and beat orders
agree across sources. **All timings and numbers are starting points to test on the footage, not standards.** Items tagged
`[craft]` are standard editing practice with thin sourcing; `[heuristic]` are synthesis, not sourced. Platform retention
figures (3 s swipe decision, loop gains) are creator/vendor claims, unverified.

## 0. Principles for every cut
- **Rule of Six** (priority when criteria conflict): emotion 51%, story 23%, rhythm 10%, eye-trace 7%, screen plane 5%, 3D space 4%.
  Give up the lowest first: break spatial continuity before rhythm, rhythm before story or emotion. If beat-sync fights the
  emotional beat, move the cut off the beat. On small vertical screens eye-trace deserves more weight than the list gives it.
  ([StudioBinder](https://www.studiobinder.com/blog/walter-murch-rule-of-six/), [No Film School](https://nofilmschool.com/2018/08/editing-eye-trace-mind-rule-six-incorrect))
- **Adjacency creates meaning.** Two shots side by side mean something neither means alone: a face then a view makes the view the
  face's reaction. This is the license to find a story in unrelated clips. ([Soundstripe](https://www.soundstripe.com/blogs/what-is-the-kuleshov-effect))
- **Cut near a blink or gaze shift** of the subject when a person is on screen. ([PremiumBeat](https://www.premiumbeat.com/blog/cutting-on-the-blink-editing-tips-from-walter-murch))
- **Plain cut is the default.** Transitions only when the footage motivates them (section B9).
- **Every shot must add information or feeling.** Cut the rest. ([invideo](https://invideo.io/blog/how-to-create-commercial-product-video/))

## A. Story structures: pick one
Timings are seconds for 15 / 30 / 60 s cuts. All are `[heuristic]` proportions on top of sourced beat orders.

| Footage has... | Use |
|---|---|
| one standout "hero" moment | A1 Hook / Build / Payoff (default) |
| a visible change or struggle | A2 Three-act or A5 Tension / Release |
| a place, a trip | A3 Journey / Arrival / Discovery |
| a person's routine, mixed light | A4 Day in the life |
| a product with a use or pain point | A6 Problem / Solution (see `product-playbook.md`) |
| pretty clips, no narrative | A7 Tonal / metric montage on music |
| continuous voice or ambient spine | A8 A-roll / B-roll |

**A1 Hook / Build / Payoff** (most footage-agnostic). Needs one hero moment + 4-8 supporting clips.
Hook = best or most surprising frame, already moving, no logo or fade-in (0-2 / 0-3 / 0-3). Context 1-2 shots (2-5 / 3-8 / 3-12).
Build = escalating shots, cuts getting shorter (5-12 / 8-24 / 12-48). Payoff = the promised moment, held about one beat longer
(12-14 / 24-28 / 48-56). Button = closing shot or loop back to the hook (last 1-4 s). Variants: *open loop* (hook, context, tension,
payoff, bonus); *step ladder* (hook, promise, three escalating micro-payoffs). Under 7 s: one idea, one hook, one payoff, stop.
Avoid when there is no hero moment (hook over-promises) or the piece is contemplative.

**A2 Three-act.** Setup ~15-20%, complication ~60% (one visible turning point), resolution ~20%. 15 s: 3/9/3. 30 s: 6/18/6.
60 s: 10/38/12. Needs a visible change of state (before/after, struggle/outcome, empty/full). Avoid for static footage.

**A3 Journey / Arrival / Discovery.** Bin clips into arrive ~20% / discover ~60% / depart ~20%. Arrival = varied establishing
wides, a threshold or transit shot, first detail. Discovery = alternate wide/medium/close, people and activity, escalate to the best
moment. Departure = quieter reflective shot (last look, golden light). 15 s: 3/9/3. 30 s: 6/18/6. 60 s: 12/36/12. Pick music early:
tempo sets pacing. Avoid when footage has no sense of place.

**A4 Day in the life.** Hook (a midday highlight or the end-of-day shot), then 3-5 moments from 6-10 clips, payoff, close. Show moments
that change across the day, not every task; give it a theme or it reads as a step list. 15 s: 2 hook + 4x3 + 1 button.
30 s: 3 + 6x4 + 3. 60 s: 4 + 8-10x4-6 (pace change at midday) + 6. Needs light/time variety.

**A5 Tension / Release.** Shorten shots progressively (or add anticipation shots: waiting, reaching, approaching), cut at the peak,
then release on a held calm wide. 15 s: 8 s at ~1.5 s shots, 3 s at ~0.5 s, 3 s hold. 30 s: 15 s at ~2, 8 s at ~1, 4 s at ~0.5, 3 s
hold. 60 s: stair of ~3 s, ~1.5 s, ~0.5 s shots, 5 s release. Avoid when nothing is at stake: manufactured tension reads fake.

**A6 Problem / Solution (product).** Hook/pain 0-2 / 0-5 / 0-6; problem 2-4 / 5-10 / 6-16; solution-demo 4-11 / 10-23 / 16-44; proof
11-13 / 23-27 / 44-54; CTA 13-15 / 27-30 / 54-60. One benefit, one job per shot. A believable "before" is required; otherwise use A2 or A7.

**A7 Montage on music.** *Metric*: cut every N beats (120 bpm, 4 beats = 2 s: 7-8 shots in 15 s, 15 in 30 s). *Tonal/thematic*: group by
shared colour, motion or light, order calm to energetic. *Intellectual*: contrasting pairs (crowd, then quiet corner), max 2-3 pairs.
*Parallel*: A/B/A/B of two threads converging in a final shot. A montage of weak shots is a slideshow.

**A8 A-roll / B-roll.** Voice or action audio is the spine; change picture every 2-4 s; each cut answers "what is being said or felt
now". Vary B-roll sizes, add close-ups. No spine audio: use A7.

**A9 Cold open.** `[craft]` 2-3 s self-explanatory climax fragment, then the main structure, then resolve the loop at the climax. Avoid when
the climax needs context or is a letdown.

## B. Sequence blocks (assembly toolkit)

**B1 Size progression.** Per location: establishing wide (3-5 s), medium action (2-5 s), close-up (1-3 s), detail (1-2 s). Group by
location, then tighten scale. Wide shows where, medium who, close what/feeling. Details are the most skipped and make it feel
considered. Only wides is monotonous. Shorten all for 9:16. ([Adobe](https://www.adobe.com/creativecloud/video/hub/guides/guide-to-sequence-shots-in-film), [Insta360](https://www.insta360.com/blog/tips/how-to-make-travel-videos.html))
*Find candidates in the map:* subject scale (face/object area fraction), camera motion, field of view.

**B2 Five-shot sequence.** Wide, medium, close-up, cutaway, reaction. Reaction and cutaway 1-2 s, others 2-4 s `[heuristic]`. Cutaways must
relate to the action. A pseudo-reaction from any face clip works if it does not contradict the action.

**B3 Variety and continuity.** Never put two near-identical framings of one subject adjacent: insert a different size or a cutaway.
Check screen direction (a subject moving right is followed by one moving left only when a reverse is intended; walking left then right
reads as going back). The 30-degree rule cannot be fixed by reorder-only editing, so use a size change, a cutaway, or hide it with a ramp.
Jump cuts are fine only when deliberate (time passing, urgency).

**B4 Match cuts.** *Action/motion* (pan, step, turn, reach continuing across the cut; cut a few frames into B's motion, most common and
invisible). *Graphic* (shared shape, line or colour at the same screen position; conspicuous, use sparingly). *Subject*, *audio bridge*.
Pairs run ~1-3 s per shot. A forced match with different speed, direction or scale looks accidental.
([StudioBinder](https://www.studiobinder.com/blog/match-cut/))

**B5 J-cut / L-cut.** J: next shot's audio starts 0.5-1 s early. L: outgoing audio spills 0.5-1 s over the next picture. Always add a short
audio crossfade. Not on every cut. Needs identifiable ambience (waves, crowd, engine). ([Vimeo](https://vimeo.com/blog/post/j-cuts-l-cuts))

**B6 Cutaways and inserts.** Face then insert of the object it looks at. 1-2 s beat between two shots that would jump-cut. Unrelated
cutaways confuse.

**B7 Reveal.** Eyeline match: a look shot (1-2 s, subject facing screen-left or right) then the view on the matching side (hold 2-4 s).
A wrong-way look breaks it. Also reveal by camera move (tilt, push, pull ending on the subject) or an object wipe uncovering the subject.

**B8 Speed ramps and slow motion.** Only native high-fps clips (60/120 fps) slow cleanly: ~40-50% for 60 fps, 25% for 120 fps, on a single
clear peak action (jump, splash, pour, turn). Ramp: normal, ease to slow at the peak, ease back. A ramp can hide a cut placed on the speed
peak. Slow beat 1-3 s, one or two ramps per sequence. 24/30 fps clips slow by interpolation badly (stutter): use a short hold instead.
Export stays 4K at the highest source fps.

**B9 Transitions.** Cinematic = in-camera or motivated: whip pan (match direction and speed; cut at the blurriest frame, ~3-6 frames
each side), object wipe (something fills the lens, cut, new shot), light flash (4-8 frames on a blown-out sun/flare), masked (foreground
element as mask, weakly sourced, test). Cheap = stock spins/glitches/stars repeated. One transition per several cuts at most.

**B10 Ordering reorder-only footage.** (1) open on an establishing wide or the A-structure hook; (2) group by location or beat;
(3) within a group tighten scale, add one cutaway/reaction; (4) check every adjacent pair for size change, angle change, screen
direction, and fix with a cutaway or a swap; (5) pair clips by motion direction so matches land on the cut; (6) alternate holds with
short beats; (7) strongest visual first or last, end on a detail or wide that resolves; (8) glue different places with J/L audio.

## C. Rhythm, sound, text, colour

**Shot length.** Reels: a visual change every ~1.5-4 s (anecdotal); 7-15 s reels reportedly retain best. Documentary-style holds 4-8 s on
observational beats and 5+ s on important travel shots. Genre ASL for context: action ~4 s, adventure ~5 s, drama ~15 s. Do not use uniform
lengths; do not go so fast the key information cannot register. Accelerating cuts (no sourced numbers, `[heuristic]`): start 2-4 beats per shot,
tighten to 1 or half-beat at the peak, hold the final shot 4+ beats.

**Beat sync.** Beat = 60/bpm s (120 bpm: 0.5 s beat, 2 s bar). Cut on bar lines for section changes; vary 1/2/4-beat lengths; hold a strong
shot 2 beats between bursts; nudge a cut 1-2 frames to lock it. Trim a long track to the cut length but keep its ending. Cutting 2-3 frames
before the beat reads as anticipation `[heuristic]`.

**Build and drop.** Riser peaks on the downbeat of the new section; impact on the drop; downlifter just after to release. Pull the music
1-2 beats (0.5-1 s) before the drop and hold the picture `[heuristic]`. Put the hero shot on the peak.

**Silence.** Never total: keep room tone or a faint ambient bed. Pull sound just before a major moment so viewers lean in. Plan it; a
wall-to-wall mix has no room for it.

**Ambience/foley.** Start each location's ambient bed a few frames before its picture. Levels are by ear (no sourced dB): ambience well below
music peak, spot foley slightly above.

**Music vs voice and ducking.** Music 10-18 dB under voice. Duck ~-12 dB subtle to -18 dB strong, ratio ~2:1, attack 15-20 ms, release 600-700 ms
(sources vary; a 1-2 dB duck is already audible).

**Loudness (Reels).** No official Meta spec. Practitioner target -14 to -16 LUFS integrated, about -1 dBTP. Check on a phone speaker. Do not
open silent: it is a cited drop-off cause.

**Hooks (first 3 s).** Most striking frame, moving, first frame works as a thumbnail; a face or hands early; one line of text stating the
promise or a curiosity gap; speech or text in the first 2 s helps (vendor claims). Mid-point beat (reveal or tempo change) at ~40-60%.

**Loop ending.** Last 1-2 s matches the first frame's framing or motion direction with a continuous audio bed; no black frame or hard stop.

**Text and captions.** Chunks of 3-7 words shown 1-3 s (0.8 s minimum). Keep reading speed under ~17 characters/second. First caption appears
with the opening image. Land text on cuts or beats. On-screen text ~25% of spoken words. Safe zone on 1080x1920: sources conflict (bottom
280-480 px, top ~220 px, right ~90 px). Keep text centred and above ~480 px from the bottom, verify on a phone.

**Colour as story.** Match shots first, then one creative grade across the piece; shift only at story turns. Warm = comfort/joy, cool or
desaturated = isolation. For HDR sources check the SDR result; see `color-and-encode.md` for conversion.

## D. Other recipes (short-form)
- **Result-first**: finished result 0-1.5 s, promise line to 3 s, chronological body, payoff last 1-2 s.
- **Before/after**: "after" flash 0-2 s, "before" with label 2-3 s, fast transformation with one cut or whip, full "after" last 2 s. Needs matched framing.
- **Process to result**: result first, 4-6 steps of 1-3 s (ramp the dead time), hold the result 1-2 s.
- **Countdown/list**: title card over the best shot, 3-6 equal-length self-contained clips of 2-4 s, number overlay and tick sound, strongest last.
- **POV**: first-person, match on movement (reach, door, turn), 1.5-3 s shots, 3+ locations, one-line premise text.
- **One-take illusion**: hide each cut on a whip pan, passing object, dark doorway or hand over the lens; cut at the blur peak with same direction
  and speed into the next clip; keep the audio bed continuous.
- **Hyper/timelapse insert**: 1-2 s tempo accelerator between real-time scenes, strong moving subject, cut on a beat. `[craft]`
- **Product hero reveal (15-30 s)**: hero + benefit line 0-3 s; 2-3 macro details 1-1.5 s each to ~8 s; in-use 8-16 s; lifestyle to ~24 s (keep the
  product readable); end card with a specific CTA 3-5 s.
- **Text-card chapters (doc/travel)**: 3-5 chapters, 2-3 s card of 3-7 words, then 3-5 shots; cards carry facts or time-of-day the footage cannot. `[craft]`

## E. Common mistakes and fixes
1. Slow or logo-first intro: start on the strongest frame, already moving.
2. No reason to stay in 3 s: add a promise or curiosity line.
3. Shots that add nothing: cut them.
4. Flat or silent audio: ambient bed, hits on cuts, a music arc.
5. Same shot size and length throughout: vary size/angle every 2-4 s.
6. Cutting on dead frames: cut on motion or at a blur peak, trim heads and tails.
7. Dead or black ending: end mid-momentum (loop or specific CTA).
8. Lifestyle-heavy product edit where the product is unclear.
9. Vague CTA: one specific action.
10. Everything under 2 s, or everything overlong: alternate fast bursts with 4-8 s holds.
11. Overused transitions and filters: hard cuts and motivated wipes; save effects for the one reveal.
